The Industrial Revolution represents a pivotal turning point in human history, fundamentally transforming the economic and social structures of Europe. Prior to this period, most manufacturing occurred in small workshops and homes, where skilled artisans produced goods using traditional hand tools. However, the introduction of steam power and mechanized production fundamentally altered this landscape. Factories emerged as the primary centers of production, concentrating labor and capital in ways previously unimaginable. Moreover, the shift from rural to urban living created unprecedented challenges for city planning and public health. It is important to note that these changes did not occur uniformly across all regions. Furthermore, historians have increasingly emphasized that the benefits of industrialization were distributed unevenly, with working-class families often enduring harsh conditions for generations before experiencing meaningful improvements in their quality of life.
