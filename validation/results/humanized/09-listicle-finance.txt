Money habits. Boring, right? But they matter more than your salary does.

Ten of them, whether you're 22 and broke or 60 and counting down to retirement. Here's the thing, though. Most people want a magic trick. There isn't one. Experts keep saying the same boring stuff, and they're right, because small moves stack up over years in a way that one big swing never does.

Start with a budget. Track what actually comes in and what goes out, and you'll find money you didn't know you were losing. Then build an emergency fund, even $500 at first, so one flat tire doesn't wreck you. Pay yourself first, meaning savings comes out before you can spend it. Pay down high-interest debt next, since a 22% credit card will eat you alive. Take the free money your employer matches in a 401(k) — that's not optional, honestly. Learn the basics of investing instead of guessing. Check your credit score once a year. Live below your means, not at the edge of them.

Get insured. Health, renters, whatever fits your life.

And be patient with yourself when you mess up. You will mess up. The people who build real wealth aren't the ones who never slip. They're the ones who start again the next month, and the month after that, for decades. That's the whole secret.
