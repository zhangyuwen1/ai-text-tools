People call compound interest the eighth wonder of the world. They're not wrong. It just means your interest starts earning its own interest, and the pile grows faster than you'd expect. Say you put in $10,000 at a 7% annual return. Year one, you've got $10,700. Year two? The interest gets figured on the whole $10,700, so you end up with $11,449. Not $11,400. Small gap, right? Over 30 years that same $10,000 grows to about $76,123, and you never added a dime. Now toss in regular contributions. $500 a month, on top of that initial $10,000, and you're looking at a portfolio north of $680,000 over those same 30 years. That's the whole trick, honestly — time does the heavy lifting. Which is exactly why financial advisors keep telling people to start early.
