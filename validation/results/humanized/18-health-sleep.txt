Good sleep is hard to get these days. Our phones don't help. Research keeps showing the same thing: sleep matters for your brain, your mood, and your body. The National Sleep Foundation says adults need seven to nine hours a night. About a third of us don't get that. Not even close. And the fallout goes way past feeling tired. Poor sleep weakens your immune system, packs on weight, and raises your risk of heart disease. It also messes with memory — your brain uses sleep to file away everything you learned that day. So what actually helps? Experts say keep a steady schedule, weekends included. Build a wind-down routine that tells your body it's time to stop. And honestly, put the phone away before bed. That blue light blocks melatonin, the stuff that makes you sleepy.
