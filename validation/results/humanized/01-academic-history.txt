The Industrial Revolution changed Europe's economy and society. That's the short version.

Before it, most stuff got made in small workshops or at home. Skilled artisans. Hand tools. Then steam power showed up, and machines took over production. Factories became the main place where things got made, pulling together workers and money in ways nobody had seen before.

People moved from farms to cities. That created big problems for city planning and public health — honestly, nobody was ready for it.

But here's the thing. These changes didn't happen the same way everywhere. And historians now stress that the gains got spread unevenly. Working-class families often lived through harsh conditions for generations before their lives actually got better.

Was it a turning point? Yeah, a huge one.
